Why You Should Stop Doomscrolling

A simple, science-backed look at how endless scrolling affects your mood, energy, and attention span—and how breaking the habit can boost your daily life.

1. It Traps Your Brain Like a Slot Machine

Ever wonder why it's so hard to put your phone down? Social media apps are designed like mini slot machines. Every time you swipe, you don't know if you'll see something funny, exciting, or upsetting.

This unpredictable reward cycle trickles dopamine into your brain, making you constantly pull the lever for "one more scroll." Recognizing this trick is the first step to regaining control over your time and focus.

2. It Constantly Drains Your Mental Energy

When reading a book or watching a movie, natural pause points—like chapters or end credits—give your brain a moment to rest and think, "Okay, time to do something else."

Infinite scrolling removes those natural stop signs. Without pause points, your brain has to manually stop itself every few seconds. This wears out your self-control, leaving you feeling tired, scattered, and unable to focus on what matters.

Practical Tips: Learn quick strategies in Psych2Go's video on How to Stop Doomscrolling (And What to Do Instead!)

3. It Feeds Bad News to Keep You Hooked

Human brains are naturally wired to pay extra attention to threats and bad news to keep us safe. App algorithms exploit this habit by serving up alarming, dramatic, or upsetting headlines to keep your eyes glued to the screen.

Stepping away from doomscrolling gives your nervous system a much-needed break—reducing stress, lowering anxiety, and making room for positive daily activities.
